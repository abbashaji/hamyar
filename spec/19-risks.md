# 19. Risks, what remains unverified, and a pre-build checklist

*Spec part 19 · extends the Founding Document (FD) risk register FD§8.2 and verification checklist FD§8.3 · uses the vocabulary of `spec/00-conventions.md` (`S0.*`) exactly. Nothing here is legal advice, and nothing here has been verified against any provider's terms or any law. Every legal or provider-dependent statement is tagged [V] for a human reviewer or for counsel.*

## 19.1 Scope, scales and ID schemes

This part does four things. (1) It carries the 15 risks of FD§8.2 forward under stable ids and adds spec-level risks: technical, legal [V], safety, operational and project (19.2, 19.3). (2) It names the tripwires that turn a risk into a concrete action (19.4). (3) It lists everything that remains unverified [V] and says where each answer is stored in the system: a `ServiceRegistryEntry` field, a `LaunchGateItem`, a configuration parameter or a counsel memo (19.5 to 19.7). (4) It gives the pre-build checklist in three separate stages, before coding starts, before the first member and before public exposure (19.8). The open decisions of `S3.*` are referenced, not restated (19.9). [D]

**Qualitative scales** (no probabilities, no invented numbers) [D]

| Scale | Labels | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Likelihood | `low`, `medium`, `high`, `unknown` | A judgment of the spec author [A]. `unknown` means nothing supports a judgment yet; it is never read as `low` and sorts as `high` |
| Impact | `minor`, `moderate`, `severe`, `critical` | `critical`: a member could be endangered, the project could be forced to close, or credit integrity is broken. `severe`: a milestone or a gate fails or trust is damaged. `moderate`: rework or a degraded measure. `minor`: cosmetic or easily reversed |
| Priority | sort by impact first, then likelihood | Two risks never get a multiplied score; labels are not numbers |
| Owner | `owner`, `founders`, `operator`, `reviewer`, `counsel`, `builder` | `owner` is the person who commissions the project; `operator` and `reviewer` are the roles of `S0.8.2`; `counsel` is an external adviser and not a system role [V]; `builder` is whoever implements |
| Status tag | `[V]`, `[H]`, `[D]`, `[P]`, `[A]` (`S0.2`) | `[V]` the risk rests on a fact a human must verify; `[H]` on a hypothesis; `[D]` it is mitigated by a binding spec decision; `[P]` a premise from the FD; `[A]` an assumption of this spec |

**ID schemes added by this part** [A] (reported as `ISS-191`): `RISK-nn` (already in `S0.5`; `RISK-01`..`RISK-15` are the FD§8.2 rows, `RISK-16` and up are spec-level), `UNV-nn` (an unverified item, 19.5), `CQ-nn` (a counsel question, 19.7), `CHK-nn` (a pre-build checklist item, 19.8), `OD-nn` (an owner decision, 19.9). Ids are never reused or renumbered.

## 19.4 Tripwires

A tripwire turns a risk into an action. It names a signal, a threshold, what the system does by itself, what a named person does, and what releases it. Tripwires are alarms, not proofs: a tripped wire says something needs a human look, never that a cause is established. [D]

**Rules for every tripwire** [D]

- A tripwire raises an `Alarm` (`alm_`, `ops` retention). A named on-call person acknowledges it. Who that person is, and the escalation interval when nobody acknowledges, are decided in the on-call procedure (`CHK-37`) and are not set here [A].
- **Tightening acts at once, loosening is slow.** Engaging a kill switch, lowering a cap or pausing a template needs one operator or reviewer. Releasing it needs two approvers (`security.two_person_actions`). Every action is written to `AdminAction`, and parameter changes also go to the public change log (`ChangeLogEntry`). Changes are prospective only (FD Principle 5).
- **Thresholds carry no invented numbers.** A threshold is one of: any occurrence (for exact-equality invariants, 0.6.1); an existing parameter of 0.10; or a level locked in a `PreRegistration` before day 1 of Phase 1 (`CHK-25`). Where a measurement is still missing, the threshold is that missing measurement and is tagged [H].
- No tripwire edits or deletes a ledger entry. Corrections are a `reversal` with `ledger.reversal_min_approvers` (RISK-16).
- No tripwire decides a legal question. A legal tripwire stops a feature and routes to `counsel` (19.7).
- Whether the system engages a kill switch by itself, or only raises the alarm, is owned by S14.*. Where a row says "automatic", the fallback if S14.* does not provide it is that the operator on call engages the switch as the first action [A].

## 19.10 What this part does and does not promise

- **Minimization, never anonymity.** The platform promises to store as little as it can (`retention.raw_ip_stored` = `false`, keyed hashes, no mandatory identity). It does not promise that a member cannot be identified, and no user-facing text says it does (`RISK-05`, `RISK-30`, `RISK-31`). [D]
- **No legal advice and no legal conclusion.** Every legal statement here is a question for counsel [V]. Nothing here has been checked against a law or a provider's terms. [D]
- **No completeness claim.** The register, the tripwires and the checklists name the risks and gaps this specification can see. Finishing the checklist does not make the project safe, lawful or sustainable; unknown risks remain, and a risk marked `[D]` is mitigated by a design decision that is itself untested until built. [D]
- **Labels are judgments.** Likelihood and impact labels are the spec author's judgments [A], not measurements, and they carry no probability. They are revised when a measurement (`CAL`, `P1M`, `SEC`, `OPS`) exists. [D]
- **Tripwires are alarms.** A tripped wire asks a human to look; it does not establish a cause. Thresholds that depend on a measurement not yet made are tagged [H]. [D]
- **The human presses Send.** No item in this part relies on automating a volunteer's account, reading a chat page, storing provider credentials or bypassing a regional restriction. Where a risk response would need that, the response is excluded (FD Principle 9, ground rules 1 and 2). [D]
- **Simulation is not evidence.** Simulation output may test the system's mechanics; it says nothing about human behavior (`RISK-43`). [D]
